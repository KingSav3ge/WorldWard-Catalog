The first alarm sounds almost polite: two clear notes from the display above the transfer-ring hatch. Conversation does not stop immediately. A person beside the notice board finishes pinning up a sheet, looks at the display, and takes the sheet down again.

The screen reads: **TRANSFER RING — PRESSURE INDICATION FALLING.**

There is no explanation beneath it. The reading may describe the air in the ring; it may describe a fault in the equipment reporting it. From here, the difference is not yet known.

Iona Vale, a station technician, looks up from the open maintenance cabinet. “Did anyone see when that started?”

Miro Sen, a medic passing through the ring, turns toward the inner hatch. Neri Kade, waiting with a travel bag, steps away from the doorway and makes room for them. No one has asked you to take charge. The warning is there, the hatch is in front of you, and the people nearby have questions of their own.

## Choose a perspective

Each introduction assumes the controlled character is present in the transfer ring and can perceive the alert. The technician variation additionally needs the selected start to establish their position at the cabinet. A selected start must establish those facts before presenting the text.

### Technician

Your hand is still on the edge of the cabinet. The display gives a compartment and a direction of change, but no cause. Iona has access to the same warning you do. You can inspect what your own position allows, ask what she has already checked, or attend to something else that seems more urgent.

**Editable intentions**

- I want to examine the warning and the information available with it.
- I ask Iona what she has already checked.
- I look for the procedure that applies to this alarm.

### Medic

Miro pauses at the hatch, watching the display rather than the people around it. A pressure warning can matter to people in several ways, but it does not establish that anyone is hurt. You can ask what Miro knows, look for someone who wants help, or seek clearer information before committing to a response.

**Editable intentions**

- I ask Miro what they have been told.
- I check on the people I can see and ask whether anyone needs help.
- I look for a route to the compartment named by the warning.

### Passenger

Neri holds the travel bag close to one leg, leaving the doorway clear. The notice tells you which compartment is reporting a change; it does not tell you whether it is safe to pass through the hatch. You can ask the crew for guidance, check the signs available to you, stay where you are, or attempt another approach.

**Editable intentions**

- I ask Iona what the warning means for people in the ring.
- I ask Neri what they saw before the alarm.
- I check the signs or instructions visible near the hatch.

These are words the player may edit and attempt. They are not pre-approved actions, automatic information, a promise of safety, or a command to another person.

## People in the opening

### Iona Vale — station technician

Iona wants a reliable reading before choosing a repair. She may have duties or information the player has not received; those details must come from the selected world state.

- “I saw the indication change. I don't know why yet.”
- “Show me what your display says, and I'll compare it with mine.”
- “I can't authorize that hatch. Ask whoever has the current watch.”
- “I'm working on this check. If you need me elsewhere, tell me what you've found.”

### Miro Sen — medic

Miro is concerned about the people in and near the ring. A title establishes neither consent nor knowledge of an unseen patient.

- “Has anyone told you they need help?”
- “I haven't examined them. I can't tell you how they are.”
- “If the way is open, I can look; I won't guess from here.”
- “Tell me exactly what you saw, not what you think it means.”

### Neri Kade — passenger

Neri wants to understand where they may go. Neri can cooperate, hesitate, decline, or pursue a different concern according to the selected state.

- “The screen changed while I was waiting. I didn't see anyone touch it.”
- “I'm not going through that hatch until someone tells me it's clear.”
- “I can show you the notice I was reading, if that helps.”
- “I don't know the station well. Which way did you come in?”
