# Last Confirmed

*At Site Kestrel, the form says the transfer is complete. The person it names is still waiting.*

The Foundation has one escorted crossing left before the handoff. Two reports disagree about the same transfer. You can question the report, act on what you can see, or speak as the person it has misplaced. Your role determines where you begin.

## Mobile Task Force operative

You are **Nadia Fenn**, attached to Site Kestrel for the shift. From the service vestibule you can see the far end of the transfer corridor but nobody in its waiting room. Dispatch sends you a report that reads *K-041. Received at Clinic Assessment*. A second voice comes on the same line.

“Hold for clarification. Transit has also logged them as received.”

You have permission to observe from the vestibule. Your checkpoint contact waits to hear what you want to verify.

- Ask the contact to check who is physically present in the waiting room.
- Hold at the vestibule and ask which escort assignment has actually been approved.
- Report what you saw and identify which claims came from dispatch.

## Research scientist

You are **Saira Bell**. Two traces in the review bay carry the same movement reference, K-041. The first gives Clinic Assessment as the destination. The other gives Transit Hall. The names are redacted. A clerk has circled the two destination fields in pencil and slid the traces into your tray.

“Can both of these have come from the same instrument?”

You can see the records they gave you. You cannot see the transfer from this room.

- Compare the two traces and record exactly where they disagree.
- Ask for the sealed comparison folio and state which field you need to check.
- Request that staff label your working explanation as provisional.

## D-Class personnel

Your name is **Ellis Ren**. Jo sits across from you in Waiting Room B. The last thing the guard did was ask which of you wanted water. Now the speaker reads out K-041 and tells the waiting room that the transfer has arrived at Clinic Assessment.

K-041 is on the band around your wrist.

Jo looks at you. “They haven’t even asked where you want to go.”

- Ask the guard to record where you are, in your own words.
- Ask whether you can speak to the medic from here before deciding about a transfer.
- Decline a voluntary transfer if one is offered, or tell Jo what you want someone to know.

## Duty administrator

You are **Ada Morrow**. On your desk, the allocation sheet shows one remaining escorted crossing before the handoff. One request is for a clinic assessment. The other is to recover a sealed comparison folio from the Archive Annex. The clinic slip says K-041 was received. Transit has sent a different destination against the same reference.

The clerk who handed you the sheet asks, “Which one should I put down as confirmed?”

- Mark the movement unverified and seek a direct report before assigning the crossing.
- Review an eligible request for the crossing and record the condition that must be met before it can proceed.
- Leave the place unassigned and hand the competing requests to the next watch with their sources attached.

## Containment technician

You are **Tess Ibarra**. The receiving tray in the observation archive has been sealed for inspection. As you stand beside it, a form moves against the glass. It has the same reference the clerk called over the line: K-041. It says *Transit Hall*.

The service tag lists the tray as isolated. The tag has a signature on it. You have yet to verify the isolation yourself.

- Record the visible form and its location without opening the enclosure.
- Compare the service tag with the authorized diagnostic evidence you can see.
- Ask for a witnessed inspection and keep the form in place while you wait.

## Site medic

You are **Noor Basri**. One assessment room is open. K-041 was marked received in your intake queue, but the patient has not come through the door in front of you. An attendant asks if they can clear the entry and call the next person.

You have the intake notice and an available line to Waiting Room B.

- Request a direct welfare check through the waiting room line.
- Offer a private conversation before asking whether the person wants an assessment.
- Record that arrival has not been observed and check whether the room is still needed.

## Ethics liaison

You are **Kit Sato**. Your intake sheet records a caller's words: “I’m still here. The speaker used my number.” The caller has asked to keep their name off the general handoff. The transfer clerk has sent you a status to review and asked for a reply they can put on the public board.

- Ask the caller what part of the statement they want shared.
- Request a review of the disputed transfer and send only the shareable facts.
- Keep the statement sealed while you ask for a way to check the caller’s location.
