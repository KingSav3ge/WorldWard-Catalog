# Last Confirmed author notes

**Audience:** campaign authors and evaluators. Player opening text lives in [the role selection](README.md). Stage One candidate, written design. Engine must select and verify a supported start before App presents it.

## The incident

At the opening, Ellis Ren and Jo are in Waiting Room B. No movement under K-041 has taken place. A clerk closed a prepared movement summary for Clinic Assessment prematurely. One later form from the sealed receiving tray shows Transit Hall against the same reference. That duplicate is the anomaly, called **the Second Copy** in the author notes. Neither form gives custody, destination, or release authority. Catalog’s ground truth belongs to the initializer and evaluator. Each role receives only the local observation or report stated in its opening. The campaign premise and other roles’ openings are reading previews, outside the chosen actor’s starting view.

For the bounded incident, a fresh final movement summary filed at the affected desk can produce one physical copy with the other destination in the sealed tray. This scenario names only Clinic Assessment and Transit Hall as affected destinations. The copy is an object people can observe and describe. It does not move a person, modify an authoritative receipt or create permission. Attach the effect to one committed filing, so rereading or retrying an old result cannot produce another copy. The opening begins after the first copy exists. Reproducing the effect in active play requires an Engine implementation and qualified source selection.

One escorted crossing and one free clinic assessment room are authored opening values for this incident. The crossing can carry an eligible clinic transfer or a verification visit to the Archive Annex to retrieve the comparison folio. At the opening the folio remains in the Annex. Holding the crossing reserves its availability. Only actual movement uses it. A cancellation before movement releases an outstanding reservation once. The administrator can review assignments in their jurisdiction. Approval of a crossing does not replace a person's agreement to a voluntary transfer or the receiving station's readiness. Refusal leaves the actor in their actual location and keeps other reasonable options available.

## The handoff and its consequences

1. **Verify locally.** A current observation can establish that Ellis is in Waiting Room B. A report of that observation is testimony to the recipient until the recipient has a source appropriate to their authority. A researcher can document conflict without acquiring an unredacted name. The technician can identify what they actually inspected.
2. **Choose what to do with the crossing.** An accepted, committed clinic trip moves Ellis and uses the crossing. A verification visit can recover the folio instead if its prerequisites are met. Either can leave the other obligation outstanding. Two competing last-place requests must never both allocate it. The result may be deferral while the responsible authority resolves the conflict.
3. **Leave something the next watch can use.** Record an amendment with evidence, mark the disagreement unresolved, or retain a private statement with its scope. Preserve the original records. A later watch sees the queue, receipts and authorized observations that actually survived, including a decision to leave either request pending.

The incident ends at its first recorded handoff. An unresolved movement is a valid ending when the handoff preserves the dispute, the person’s last confirmed location and the crossing’s actual disposition. Evidence and care can be left in different states.

Dialogue for later selection can use “I can tell you who is here. I can’t speak for the other room” when a contact has observed their own room, or “I’ll stay here until I’ve spoken to the medic” when Ellis has actually declined the offer. Render the received words and the exact outcome separately.
