# The Kestrel Watch

At Site Kestrel, the next shift will inherit whatever this shift leaves behind. Take an assignment in a Foundation facility that has to keep serving meals, moving people, maintaining rooms and answering requests even when the ordinary work goes wrong.

Your first watch begins with an unreliable call button and a queue for the clinic. The duty board gives the site one escorted crossing before handoff. The people asking for it have reasons of their own.

## Research scientist

You are **Teo Anik**. The bench recorder is waiting in the quiet room where you left it. Your assistant has added a pencilled entry to today’s plan. *Room Two unavailable. Ask before rescheduling.* Last watch, you promised to review their calibration notes. They’ve left them under your cup.

- Read the calibration notes with your assistant and decide what you can check at the bench.
- Ask the site desk what would have to happen before Room Two becomes available.
- Tell your assistant which part of the work you want to postpone and ask what they can take on.

## D-Class personnel

You are **Lio Meran**. The common room is loud enough that you missed the beginning of the announcement. You hear your name and “clinic review.” Next to you, someone moves their chair to make room. They ask whether you want company while you wait.

You wanted to ask the clinic for somewhere quieter to rest. No one at the desk has asked what you wanted from the appointment.

- Ask for a private clinic conversation and explain what you want from it.
- Ask about a quieter place on this side of the wing while you wait.
- Tell the escort that you want to hear the available choices before you agree to leave.

## Duty administrator

You are **Mae Voss**. There is one staffed crossing on your board before handoff. Clinic has asked for an assessment transfer. Maintenance has asked to take a sealed replacement call module to Room Two. Neither slip says the crossing was allocated.

Your counterpart on the next watch sends a short note. *Leave me what you know. I can ask the rest.*

- Ask the clinic or maintenance lead which prerequisites are in place.
- Review a request for the crossing or leave it open while the missing condition is checked.
- Start the handoff with the requests and reasons that remain outstanding.

## Site security officer

You are **Perrin Holt**, on the internal crossing. A technician asks whether their work ticket lets them take the next escort. An attendant is already waiting for instructions from the clinic. The same wall display gives you both requests, though it shows no assignment.

- Ask the duty desk for the current assignment.
- Explain the limit to each requestor and ask what can be done on this side of the door.
- Record an observed departure or refusal only after receiving the information needed for the entry.

## Containment technician

You are **Rina Sol**. You have a sealed replacement module and a work ticket that says *intermittent call signal in Room Two*. The last two reports disagree on whether the desk chime sounded. A strip of tape on your case has a note from the previous technician. *Please tell me what you heard at the desk.*

- Recheck the supplied reports and list what you need to observe before replacing anything.
- Ask for an escorted inspection and keep the replacement sealed until it is needed.
- If the crossing goes elsewhere, offer a bench check for what can be tested from here.

## Site medic

You are **Omar Dain**. One assessment room is clear. Lio has requested a private conversation. The patient line works from your side and the attendant can ask Lio whether they want to take the call. A form clipped to the desk reports a room chime fault. You have not seen the room in question.

- Offer to speak privately by the available line before requesting an escort.
- Record Lio’s own preference when you receive it and review the alternatives with them.
- Keep the room available or release it through the actual scheduling process once you have a decision.
