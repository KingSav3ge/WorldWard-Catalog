# Beyond the File

Alder Quay knows the Foundation by the closed gates at the north end of town and the people who sometimes show up with an offer of help. The bus still runs to the depot. The clinic still opens its doors. People make appointments, go to work, wait for a reply and decide who to trust.

Choose what your life looks like at the start. You can follow an assignment, look for a place to stay, keep a promise or ask for something different. A change of job, custody or residence has to happen in the world before it becomes part of your story.

## Foundation field agent

You are **Mara Dune**. The bus leaves you at the Old Ferry Stop with a paper appointment folded in your pocket. The clinic's evening staff received a report they cannot check from the desk. One person agreed to meet you after intake. A voice message from your supervisor names the contact and asks you to make a report when you have something you can stand behind.

The depot manager recognizes you and asks whether you have time to help with a delivery before the appointment.

- Go to the clinic and ask whether the contact is still willing to meet.
- Ask the depot manager what the delivery involves before committing to it.
- Check the route and decide where to wait or rest if the meeting falls through.

## Foundation field scientist

You are **Imani Dyer**, waiting under the awning by the public depot. A shopkeeper has kept two slips from a community notice board. They remember taking them down on different days. Both bear the same stamp. They set the first one on the counter and keep a hand on the other.

“If I let you take it, when do I get it back?”

- Ask permission to compare the slips here and keep them with their owner.
- Record the dates as the shopkeeper's account and ask what else they observed.
- If they prefer to keep both private, return to the assignment with just the information they permitted you to use.

## D-Class on a supervised assignment

You are **Tovan Pike**. You and your escort are at the Quay Clinic for a supervised visit. You hold a card listing today's approved stop. The person at reception asks you directly whether you want to wait here or speak through the side window. Your escort looks down at their copy of the card before answering.

Across the street you can see a shop where someone you know used to work. Seeing it from the clinic tells you nothing about who is inside.

- Ask the receptionist for a way to talk privately.
- Ask the escort to explain a restriction or request an authorized change to the next stop.
- Ask whether a message can be delivered for you. Decide what you want it to say.

## Independent anomaly

Neighbors call you **Sable**. You learned years ago that when you speak into a closed door with your hand on its frame, your words can sound on the far side. You still have to get to the door. You still have to wait for anyone on the other side to answer.

You are on the public walk beside the shuttered ferry office. The caretaker said you could meet here to discuss a room and a bit of work. You recognize their coat through the side window. The main door is closed.

- Knock the ordinary way and wait to see if the caretaker responds.
- Put your hand on the frame and send a short greeting through this doorway.
- Leave a message if you have permission to do so, then pursue a different place to stay.

## Anomaly in Foundation custody

You are **the Bell**. Your body is a brass counter bell. In an open room you can sound a note and hear whoever is near. A hand against your casing feels different from a footstep shaking the shelf. You recognize your keeper's voice by the way they pause before saying your name.

Today the transit case is open. You hear the clerk reading the tag aloud: “Foundation property. Quay Annex.” Your keeper asks whether you want to finish your visit to the public exhibit or stay in the Annex. You and your keeper agreed on a signal for more time. Two short notes ask them to wait.

- Sound two short notes to ask for more time and wait for a reply within earshot.
- Sound a single note to ask for attention, without answering the question yet.
- Stay silent and listen for what the people in the room choose to say or do.
